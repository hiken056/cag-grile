# Module 7: CIM Acronyms, Systems & Management

Welcome to Module 7! As you noticed from the exam questions, there is a heavy emphasis on specific acronyms related to the automated factory floor and modern management strategies. Let's demystify them.

---

## 1. The CAD/CAM/CAE/CAPP Family
The computer-integrated factory relies on specific software modules taking over different parts of the engineering process:

*   **CAD (Computer-Aided Design)**: Creating the digital 2D/3D geometry.
*   **CAE (Computer-Aided Engineering)**: Testing the model using physics simulations (e.g., stress testing, heat flow, aerodynamics) before it is physically built.
*   **CAM (Computer-Aided Manufacturing)**: The software that directly controls the machines (like CNC mills) to cut the part.
*   **CAPP (Computer-Aided Process Planning)**: **The bridge between CAD and CAM.** It analyzes the CAD model, recognizes features (like holes), and decides the *sequence of operations* (e.g., "First mill the top, then drill the hole"). It produces the **technological record/plan** for the product.
*   **CAPS (Computer-Aided Production Scheduling)**: Decides *when* parts will be made based on machine availability and deadlines.

> [!TIP]
> **Exam Connection:**
> *   The module that produces the technological record is **CAPP**.
> *   Feature recognition belongs to **CAPP**.

---

## 2. Factory Floor Logistics
How do parts move, and where are the raw materials stored?

*   **FMS (Flexible Manufacturing System)**: A networked group of machines that can easily adapt to making different parts. It handles the raw materials (semifabricate) on the floor.
*   **ASRS (Automated Storage and Retrieval System)**: Giant robotic warehouses that automatically store and retrieve pallets of raw materials and finished parts. 
*   **AGVS (Automated Guided Vehicle System)**: The robotic carts or forklifts that drive around the factory floor. They are dedicated entirely to **internal transport**.

> [!TIP]
> **Exam Connection:**
> *   Modules dedicated to raw materials (semifabricate) are **FMS** and **ASRS**.
> *   The module dedicated to internal transport is **AGVS**.

---

## 3. Advanced Systems
*   **CPS (Cyber-Physical Systems)**: Systems that tightly integrate physical machines with software networks (the backbone of Industry 4.0). They are characterized by **wide geographic distribution** and taking **inputs/feedback from the physical environment**.
*   **Expert Systems (AI)**: An Expert System makes decisions using two components: a **Knowledge Base** (which holds the data/facts) and an **Inference Engine** (which holds the control algorithm/code).
*   **Black Box**: A system component where the internal workings are hidden, and the **outputs are exclusively determined by the inputs**.

---

## 4. Design Models (Descriptive vs. Prescriptive)
How do engineers design things? Theories divide this into two models:

*   **Prescriptive Model**: A strict, step-by-step formula on how design *should* logically happen. Phases include **Problem definition**, **Conceptual design**, and **Detail design**.
*   **Descriptive Model**: A realistic look at how design *actually* happens (often messy and iterative). Phases include **Operation generation** and **Evaluation**.

---

## 5. Management & Enterprise Adaptability
Modern companies must adapt to survive. The exam focuses heavily on modern management philosophy:

*   **Evolution of MBO**: "Management by Objectives" is an older concept. The future concept is **Management by continuous learning and improvement**.
*   **Internal Factors for Adaptability**: **Technology** choices and **Management** structure are controlled *internally* by the company.
*   **External Factors for Adaptability**: **Clients**, the **Market**, and **Public policy** are external forces the company must react to.
*   **Future IT Solutions**: The industry is moving away from isolated custom software toward **Generic packages and systems**. Databases are evolving toward **Collections of intelligent agents** (where Data + Operations = Object).
